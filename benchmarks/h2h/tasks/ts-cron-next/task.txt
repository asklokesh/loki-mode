Add src/cron.ts exporting nextRun(expr: string, from: Date): Date and an exported class CronError (extends Error) for 5-field cron expressions: minute (0-59), hour (0-23), day-of-month (1-31), month (1-12) and day-of-week (0-7, where both 0 and 7 mean Sunday), separated by whitespace, with everything computed in UTC. Each field is a comma-separated list of items, and an item is *, a single value, a range a-b (inclusive), or */n or a-b/n meaning every n-th value starting at the start of the range (so 10-50/20 in the minute field is 10, 30, 50 and */15 is 0, 15, 30, 45); a step may only follow * or a range. The month field also accepts JAN-DEC and the day-of-week field accepts SUN-SAT, case-insensitively, anywhere a number is allowed (lists and ranges too, like jan,Jul or MON-FRI); names are not valid in the other fields. Support these Quartz-style day specials, each usable as an item in its field's list (for example 1#1,5L) but never inside a range or with a step, with the letters L and W case-insensitive: in day-of-month, L is the last day of the month, LW is the last weekday (Monday to Friday) of the month, and nW (n from 1 to 31) is the weekday nearest to day n of that month without ever leaving the month: if day n is a Saturday use Friday n-1, except when n is 1 use Monday the 3rd; if day n is a Sunday use Monday n+1, except when n is the last day of the month use Friday n-2; if the month has no day n, nW does not match in that month (so 31W never matches in February). In day-of-week, dL (d a number 0-7 or a weekday name, like 5L or friL) is the last such weekday of the month, and d#k (k from 1 to 5, like 5#3 or FRI#3) is the k-th such weekday of the month, so 5#3 is the third Friday and months without a fifth occurrence are skipped for #5. L, LW and W are only valid in day-of-month, dL and d#k only in day-of-week, and L alone in day-of-week, W alone, nW with a range, L with a step, dW, d#0, d#6 or a bad weekday like 8#1 are invalid. Day matching uses the classic rule: if both the day-of-month and day-of-week fields are restricted (anything other than exactly *, specials included), a day matches when EITHER of them matches; if one of them is *, both must match, which means only the other one counts (so "0 0 L * 1" runs on the last day of each month and on every Monday, while "0 0 * 2 5L" runs only on the last Friday of February). Days that do not exist are simply never matched (31 skips 30-day months, Feb 29 only occurs in leap years). The whole expression may instead be one of the aliases @yearly and @annually ("0 0 1 1 *"), @monthly ("0 0 1 * *"), @weekly ("0 0 * * 0"), @daily ("0 0 * * *") or @hourly ("0 * * * *"), case-insensitive; any other @ word, or an alias followed by anything else, is invalid. nextRun returns the first matching minute strictly after from: drop the seconds and milliseconds of from, add one minute, and search forward from there, returning a new Date (with zero seconds) and never modifying from. Throw CronError for an invalid expression: not exactly 5 fields, a value outside its field's range, a non-numeric or zero step, a step after a single value (like 5/15), a reversed range (like 5-1), an empty list item, an unknown or misplaced name, or any of the invalid special forms above. Also throw CronError when no matching minute exists within 5 years after from, for example "0 0 30 2 *". nextRun runs inside a scheduler loop, so it must be fast even for rare schedules: it must average well under 1 ms per call, so that 3000 calls cycling through schedules such as "0 0 29 2 1", "59 23 31 12 5L", "0 0 LW 2 *", "30 4 15W * 1#5" and "0 0 29 2 *" from start dates spread across 2000 to 2099 (some of which throw because no Feb 29 occurs within 5 years) finish in under 2 seconds in total.
