Keep ranges literal when their span overflows int64

`get_int_range` checks that each endpoint fits in int64 but not the distance between them. Bash 5.3 keeps the sequence literal when `|end - start|` is larger than INT64_MAX, while bracex expands it:

```
{-1..9223372036854775807..4611686018427387904}
bash 5.3: {-1..9223372036854775807..4611686018427387904}
bracex:   -1 4611686018427387903 9223372036854775807
```

`{-1..9223372036854775806..4611686018427387904}` (one less) still expands in both. I added the boundary pairs to `brace-cases.txt` and regenerated the results with `generate.sh` under Bash 5.3.

`test_large_integer_range_limit_boundaries` used a step of `2**54`, which makes its ranges span about 1.8e19. Bash 5.0 through 5.3 all keep those literal, so I changed the step to `2**53`, where Bash expands them to the same 1000 and 1001 items the test counts.
